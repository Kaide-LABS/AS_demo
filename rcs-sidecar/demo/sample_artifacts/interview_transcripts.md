# Sustainable Packaging Attitudes Study — In-Depth Interview Transcripts
**Meridian Insights Group · Study MIG-SP-2026-Q1**
*Interviews conducted February 4–21, 2026 · Moderator: L. Okafor · Platform: Zoom, recorded with consent*

---

## Transcript 1 — R-0007

**Segment: Conviction Buyer · 36 / Female / South / $100k–$125k / Household of 4**
*Recorded February 6, 2026 · Duration 34 min · Excerpted*

**Interviewer:** Walk me through the last grocery trip where you thought about packaging.

**Respondent:** Honestly, every grocery trip. Last Saturday I was at the co-op picking up laundry detergent, and I spent probably five minutes comparing three bottles. One of them had a leaf and said "eco-formula" and nothing else — I put that right back. The second one had a recycled-content percentage printed on the back, forty-three percent post-consumer. I bought that one.

**Interviewer:** What made the difference?

**Respondent:** Specificity. If a brand isn't putting their certifications on the front, I assume they don't have any. I'm happy to pay more — I paid almost three dollars more for that detergent — but it has to be real. The generic "green" stuff actually makes me angrier than a regular bottle that doesn't claim anything, because it's manipulative.

**Interviewer:** Where do you learn about brands that meet your standards?

**Respondent:** Instagram, mostly. There are a few creators I follow who test products and call out greenwashing. I also read the B Corp directory when I'm curious about a new brand. My husband thinks I'm insane about this.

**Interviewer:** Has anything in the last year changed your shopping?

**Respondent:** I stopped buying from two brands I used to love because their packaging got worse, not better. One of them switched from glass back to plastic during the supply-chain issues and never switched back. I felt betrayed, genuinely.

**Interviewer:** What would make you trust a new brand?

**Respondent:** Certifications I recognize. FSC for paper. How2Recycle labels. A specific number — not "sustainable" but "45% recycled, third-party verified in 2025." And I want to see the certification number or the audit year on the pack. If you can't put the number on the box, I don't believe you.

**Interviewer:** What about price?

**Respondent:** I'll pay up to about twenty percent more for something I trust. Beyond that it gets hard to justify. But within that range, I'm genuinely not price-sensitive. I'd rather buy less of something good than more of something questionable.

**Interviewer:** How do your kids factor in?

**Respondent:** We have two, eight and five. The older one watches the labels now because we've talked about it. She asked me last week why some yogurts have a recycling number and some don't. That's a win for me.

**Interviewer:** Anything you wish brands would stop doing?

**Respondent:** Using the color green and a leaf and thinking that's enough. Put a leaf on a box of anything and a lot of people will buy it — but people like me, we've learned to treat the leaf as a warning sign.

## Transcript 2 — R-0044

**Segment: Pragmatic Parent · 35 / Female / South / $100k–$125k / Household of 4**
*Recorded February 11, 2026 · Duration 29 min · Excerpted*

**Interviewer:** Tell me about how you think about packaging when you shop.

**Respondent:** I think about it, but I'm not going to lie — it's one thing on a long list. I've got four kids. I'm buying milk, cereal, snacks for lunchboxes, detergent, toilet paper. If the sustainable version is within a dollar or two, I'll grab it. But I've got four kids and a budget, so I'm not going to double my grocery bill for a principle.

**Interviewer:** So price parity matters?

**Respondent:** Price parity and availability. If it's sitting right next to the regular version and it's the same price or close, I'll pick it up most of the time. If I have to go to a specialty store or pay twice as much, it's not happening in a normal week. Maybe for special occasions.

**Interviewer:** Where do you learn about sustainable options?

**Respondent:** Honestly, the Target app. I do most of my shopping there. They have a little leaf icon on some products now, and I notice it. Sometimes my sister will send me something she likes and I'll try it. I don't follow sustainability accounts on Instagram or anything like that.

**Interviewer:** What about health considerations?

**Respondent:** Oh, that's huge for me. BPA-free, phthalate-free, I read those labels carefully because of the kids. And honestly, a lot of the time the healthier option and the more sustainable option are the same product. So it kind of comes together.

**Interviewer:** Have you switched brands in the last year?

**Respondent:** Yeah, I switched laundry detergent because the refill pouch my old brand sold got discontinued. That was annoying. I went to a brand that still does refills. It wasn't really about sustainability as much as I just liked the system — less plastic to throw out, and it was cheaper per load too.

**Interviewer:** Do you trust the sustainability claims you see on products?

**Respondent:** Mostly? I don't read them super carefully. I think I trust the big retailers' labels more than I trust brand-specific claims. If Costco says something is sustainable I tend to take it at face value, which I know is maybe not rational. A random brand I've never heard of — I'm more skeptical.

**Interviewer:** If a brand wanted to reach you, what would work?

**Respondent:** Be cheap enough that I don't have to think about it. Put it on the shelf next to the regular version. Make the refill easy. Don't lecture me about the planet — I know, everybody knows. Just make it not harder.

## Transcript 3 — R-0078

**Segment: Skeptical Shopper · 48 / Male / Northeast / $75k–$100k / Household of 3**
*Recorded February 17, 2026 · Duration 41 min · Excerpted*

**Interviewer:** What's your general feeling about sustainable packaging?

**Respondent:** My general feeling is that most of it is marketing. I'm not against sustainability. I recycle, I've driven a Prius for twelve years, I'm not some kind of denialist. But when I see a box that says "eco-friendly" in green letters, I assume I'm being sold something that probably isn't meaningfully different from the conventional version. Companies figured out people will pay more for that word, so now everything says it.

**Interviewer:** What changes your mind about a specific product?

**Respondent:** Put the certification number on it. Tell me who audited you and when. Anybody can slap a green leaf on a box. If you're FSC-certified, put the number there. If you're B Corp, put the year. If your packaging is 60% recycled content, say exactly that, and tell me who verified it. Then I'll listen.

**Interviewer:** Where do you go for trustworthy information?

**Respondent:** Consumer Reports, mostly. Long-form journalism. I read labels carefully — I probably spend more time reading packaging than the average person. I'm not on social media for this. The last thing I trust is some influencer telling me a brand is good.

**Interviewer:** Have you ever returned a product because of packaging?

**Respondent:** Yes, a few times. I returned a supplement last year because they made a big deal about being sustainable, and when I got it the supplement was in a plastic bottle wrapped in another plastic sleeve inside a plastic-lined box. That's insulting. I emailed the company and they sent back a form letter. I haven't bought from them since.

**Interviewer:** Would you pay more for packaging you trust?

**Respondent:** I have. Maybe ten percent, fifteen on a good day. But the ten percent has to come with evidence. I'm not paying a premium for a claim I can't verify. That's a tax on trust and I don't have that kind of trust to give.

**Interviewer:** Any advice you'd give to brands in this space?

**Respondent:** Stop selling feelings. Sell verifiable facts. I'm a boring customer, I know. But I've been burned enough times that the only path back to my wallet is specificity. A year. A number. A third party. Anything else is noise.

**Interviewer:** Last question — do you think this will change?

**Respondent:** I think it has to. People younger than me are going to demand more evidence, not less. The "trust us, we're green" era is ending. Either the industry self-regulates or the FTC does it for them. I'd prefer the former but I'll take the latter.
